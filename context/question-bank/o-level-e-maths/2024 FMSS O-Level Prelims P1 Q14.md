## Question

> **Marks:** (a) 2, (b) 4; total 6
> **Page:** 11

$ABC$ is an equilateral triangle with lengths $(5x+y)\text{ cm}$, $(16y-x)\text{ cm}$ and $(4x+5y-3)\text{ cm}$.

**(a)** Write down two simultaneous equations, in terms of $x$ and $y$, to represent this information. [2]

**(b)** Solve the simultaneous equations and hence find the area of triangle $ABC$. [4]

## Answer Scheme

**(a)** Any two of

$$
5x+y = 16y-x, \quad 5x+y = 4x+5y-3, \quad 16y-x = 4x+5y-3.
$$

B1, B1.

**(b)**

$$
6x = 15y \quad (1)
$$

$$
x = 4y-3 \quad (2)
$$

Substitute $(2)$ into $(1)$:

$$
6(4y-3) = 15y, \quad 24y-18 = 15y, \quad y = 2.
$$

$$
x = 4(2)-3 = 5.
$$

$$
AC = 4(5)+5(2)-3 = 27\text{ cm}.
$$

Perpendicular height from $B$ to $AC$:

$$
\sqrt{27^{2}-\left(\frac{27}{2}\right)^{2}} = 23.382\text{ cm}.
$$

$$
\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2} \times 27 \times 23.382 = 316\text{ cm}^{2} \text{ (3 s.f.)}.
$$

Or, using $\angle ABC = 60^{\circ}$:

$$
\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2} \times 27 \times 27 \times \sin 60^{\circ} = 316\text{ cm}^{2} \text{ (3 s.f.)}.
$$

M1 for substitution or elimination. A1, A1 for $x = 5$, $y = 2$. A1 for the area.

## Question

> **Marks:** 6
> **Page:** 18

In the diagram, $ABCDE$ is a regular pentagon.

![Regular pentagon ABCDE with diagonal BE](../../subjects/o-level-e-maths/past-year-papers/2024/ACSBR/images/ACSBR-2024-4E5N-Prelim-Math-P1-QP-pages/page-18.png)

(a) Show that $BE$ is parallel to $CD$.

(b) The sides $AB$ and $DC$ are produced to meet at $X$.
What type of quadrilateral is $BECX$?
Explain your answer.

## Answer Scheme

(a) Each interior angle of the regular pentagon is

$$
\angle EDC = \angle BAE = \frac{(5-2)\times 180^{\circ}}{5} = 108^{\circ}.
$$

$$
\angle AEB = \frac{180^{\circ}-108^{\circ}}{2} = 36^{\circ}
$$

$$
\angle BED = 108^{\circ}-36^{\circ} = 72^{\circ}
$$

$$
\angle BED+\angle EDC = 72^{\circ}+108^{\circ} = 180^{\circ}
$$

By the converse of co-interior angles, $BE \parallel CD$.

(b) $\angle EBX = 180^{\circ}-36^{\circ} = 144^{\circ}$.

$\angle BEC = 108^{\circ}-36^{\circ}-36^{\circ} = 36^{\circ}$.

As $\angle EBX+\angle BEC = 180^{\circ}$, by the converse of co-interior angles, $EC \parallel BX$.

$BECX$ is a rhombus as $BE \parallel CX$ (or $DX$) and $EC \parallel BX$, and adjacent sides $BE = EC$.

Or an equivalent method with correct reasoning (for example $BE \parallel CX$ and a pair of opposite equal angles).

## Question

> **Marks:** (a) 2, (b) 3, (c) 1; total 6
> **Page:** 20–21

The first four terms in a sequence of numbers, $T_{1}$, $T_{2}$, $T_{3}$, $T_{4}$, $\ldots$ are given as shown.

$$
\begin{aligned}
T_{1} &= 1+2+4=7 \\
T_{2} &= 2+5+9=16 \\
T_{3} &= 4+8+16=28 \\
T_{4} &= 8+11+25=44
\end{aligned}
$$

**(a)** Find an expression, in terms of $n$, for the $n$th term, $T_{n}$, of the sequence. [2]

**(b)** Show that $T_{n}-T_{n-1}=2^{n-2}+2n+4$. [3]

**(c)** Hence, explain why the difference between consecutive terms in the sequence is always an even number. [1]

## Answer Scheme

**(a)**

$$
T_{n}=2^{n-1}+(3n-1)+(n+1)^{2}
$$

B1: all 3 terms correct. B1: Any correct term in the given space.

**(b)**

$$
\begin{aligned}
T_{n}-T_{n-1} &= 2^{n-1}+(3n-1)+(n+1)^{2}-\left[2^{n-2}+(3(n-1)-1)+n^{2}\right] \\
&= 2^{n-1}+3n-1+n^{2}+2n+1-2^{n-2}-3n+4-n^{2} \\
&= 2^{n-1}+2n-2^{n-2}+4 \\
&= 2^{n-2}(2-1)+2n+4 \\
&= 2^{n-2}+2n+4
\end{aligned}
$$

M1: Replace $n$ by $n-1$. M1: expansion. M1.

**(c)** Since the difference of the consecutive terms could be expressed as $2(2^{n-3}+n+2)$ and $n$ must be a natural number/positive integer, the difference is always an even number. B1: must take out factor of 2.

> **Markers Report:** This is a hence question. There is a condition that $n>2$ missing.

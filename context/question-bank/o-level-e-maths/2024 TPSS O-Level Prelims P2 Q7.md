## Question

> **Marks:** 11
> **Page:** 16–17

(a) Complete the table of values for $y = 10-x-\dfrac{16}{x}$. [1]

| $x$ | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $y$ | $-7$ | 0 | 1.7 |  | 1.8 | 1.3 | 0.7 | 0 | $-0.8$ |

(b) On the grid opposite, draw the graph of $y = 10-x-\dfrac{16}{x}$ for $1 \le x \le 9$. [3]

The grid has $x$ from 0 to 9 and $y$ from $-8$ to 3.

![Blank graph grid for Question 7](../../subjects/o-level-e-maths/past-year-papers/2024/TPSS/images/2024-TPSS-EMATH-P2-PRELIM-QP-pages/page-17.png)

(c) By drawing a tangent, find the gradient of the curve at $x = 3$. [2]

(d) Use your graph to solve $9-x-\dfrac{16}{x} = 0$. [2]

(e) The straight line $y = mx-1$ intersects the curve $y = 10-x-\dfrac{16}{x}$ at two points.

(i) Write down and simplify the equation, in terms of $m$, in the form $Ax^{2}+Bx+16 = 0$, which is satisfied by the values of $x$ at the points of intersection. [2]

(ii) If the values of $y$ at the points of intersection are greater than or equal to zero, find the least value of $m$. [1]

## Answer Scheme

(a) $2$. B1

(b) Smooth curve through 9 correct points. B3.

Or B2 follow through for 9 points correct. Or B1 follow through for 7 or 8 points correct.

The scheme draws the curve from below $y = -8$ near $x = 1$, through $(2, 0)$, a maximum near $x = 4$, and down through $(8, 0)$ to about $(9, -0.8)$. A tangent is drawn at $x = 3$. A straight line through the curve is also shown.

![Scheme graph of y = 10 - x - 16/x with a tangent at x = 3](../../subjects/o-level-e-maths/past-year-papers/2024/TPSS/images/2024-TPSS-EMATH-P2-PRELIM-MS-pages/page-07.png)

(c) Gradient value between 0.6 and 0.9. The exact value is $\dfrac{7}{9} = 0.\dot{7}$.

M1 for a tangent line at $x = 3$. A1

(d)

$$
9-x-\frac{16}{x} = 0
$$

$$
10-x-\frac{16}{x} = 1
$$

$y = 1$.

$x = [2.3$ to $2.5]$ or $x = [6.5$ to $6.7]$. A1, A1

(e) (i)

$$
10-x-\frac{16}{x} = mx-1
$$

$$
10x-x^{2}-16 = mx^{2}-x \quad \text{M1 (elimination of the fraction)}
$$

$$
mx^{2}+x^{2}-11x+16 = 0
$$

$$
(m+1)x^{2}-11x+16 = 0 \quad \text{B1}
$$

(ii) $\dfrac{1}{2}$. B1

## Question

> **Marks:** 8
> **Page:** 18–19

The masses, in grams, of a batch of 300 eggs are recorded.
The cumulative frequency curve shows the distribution of the masses.

![Cumulative frequency curve of egg masses from 30 g to 70 g](../../subjects/o-level-e-maths/past-year-papers/2024/TPSS/images/2024-TPSS-EMATH-P2-PRELIM-QP-pages/page-18.png)

(a) Use the curve to find

(i) the median mass of the eggs, [1]

(ii) the interquartile range, [2]

(iii) the 10th percentile. [1]

(b) An egg is classified as 'Medium' if its mass is greater than or equal to 50 g but less than 56 g. Two eggs are chosen at random.
Calculate the probability that both are 'Medium' eggs. [2]

(c) The masses, in grams, of a second batch of 300 eggs are recorded and are represented by the box-and-whisker diagram below. The scale runs from 20 g to 80 g.

![Box-and-whisker diagram of the second batch of eggs](../../subjects/o-level-e-maths/past-year-papers/2024/TPSS/images/2024-TPSS-EMATH-P2-PRELIM-QP-pages/page-19.png)

(i) Which batch of the eggs has more consistent mass?
Justify your answer using appropriate figures. [1]

(ii) The right whisker is longer than the left whisker.
Explain what this tells us about the distribution of the data set. [1]

## Answer Scheme

(a) (i) 48 g. B1

(ii) $51-45$ or $51.5-45$.

$= 6$ g or $= 6.5$ g.

B1 for the lower quartile or the upper quartile. A1

(iii) 40 g. B1

(b) $260-200 = 60$.

$$
\frac{60}{300} \times \frac{59}{299} = \frac{59}{1495}
$$

M1, A1. Accept 0.0395.

(c) (i) 1st batch of eggs: interquartile range $= 6$ g (6.5 g).

2nd batch of eggs: interquartile range $= 10$ g. B1

Since $6$ g $< 10$ g, the mass of the 1st batch varies less widely, hence the mass is more consistent.

(ii) The mass of the top 25% varies more widely than the bottom 25%. B1

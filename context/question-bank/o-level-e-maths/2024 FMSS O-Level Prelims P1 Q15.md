## Question

> **Marks:** (a) 3, (b) 2; total 5
> **Page:** 12

**(a)** Given that $\dfrac{\left(4^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{y+1}}{16} = 8^{2-y}$, find the value of $y$. [3]

**(b)** Jasmine says that $2^{500}$ is greater than $5^{250}$. Do you agree with her statement?
Justify your answer with mathematical working. [2]

## Answer Scheme

**(a)**

$$
\frac{\left(4^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{y+1}}{16} = 8^{2-y}
$$

$$
2^{\frac{1}{2} \cdot 4(y+1)} = 2^{3(2-y)}
$$

using $4^{\frac{1}{2}} = 2$, or $2^{4(y+1)}$, or $2^{3(2-y)}$, or $2^{1-(4y+4)}$.

$$
1-(4y+4) = 6-3y
$$

$$
1-4y-4 = 6-3y, \quad y = -9.
$$

M1, M1, A1.

**(b)**

$$
2^{500} = (2^{2})^{250} = 4^{250}.
$$

$4^{250} < 5^{250}$. I disagree with her claim because $4^{250} < 5^{250}$.

M1 for $(2^{2})^{250}$ or $4^{250}$. AG1 must state $4^{250} < 5^{250}$ or equivalent.

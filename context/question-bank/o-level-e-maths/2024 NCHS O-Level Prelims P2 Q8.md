## Question

> **Marks:** (a)(i) [1]; (a)(ii) [2]; (b) [2]; (c) [2]; (d) [1]
> **Page:** 16–17

The cumulative frequency curve shows the distribution of the lengths (cm) of 800 lobsters caught in May during a lobster fishing trip.

![Cumulative frequency curve of lobster lengths](../../subjects/o-level-e-maths/past-year-papers/2024/NCHS/images/2024-NCHS-Prelim-Math-P2-QP-pages/page-16.png)

**(a)** Use the graph to estimate

**(i)** the median length,

**(ii)** the interquartile range.

**(b)** To ensure sustainability of the lobster population, it is illegal to catch lobsters shorter than 8.3 cm or longer than 12.7 cm.
Estimate the percentage of lobsters that needs to be released.

**(c)** The lengths of another 800 lobsters caught in June are recorded.
The box-and-whisker plot shows the distribution of the lengths.

![Box-and-whisker plot of June lobster lengths](../../subjects/o-level-e-maths/past-year-papers/2024/NCHS/images/2024-NCHS-Prelim-Math-P2-QP-pages/page-17.png)

Make two comments comparing the lengths of the lobsters caught in May and June.
Use figures to support your answers.

**(d)** Jill makes a claim that “fishermen are able to keep a larger percentage of their lobsters in June as compared to May”. Do you agree?

## Answer Scheme

**(a) (i)** Middle position $= 400$th. Median length $= 10.6$ cm.

**(ii)** $Q_{1}$ (200th) $= 9.95$, $Q_{3}$ (600th) $= 11.25$ or $11.2$.

Interquartile range $= 1.3$ cm or $1.25$ cm.

**(b)** $\dfrac{15+25}{800}\times 100\% = 5\%$, or $\dfrac{20+25}{800}\times 100\% = 5.625\%$.

**(c)** Lobsters caught in June are shorter in length as the median length of 9.3 cm is lower as compared to 10.6 cm in May.

The lengths of the lobsters caught in June have a wider spread as the interquartile range of 3.7 cm is higher as compared to 1.3 cm in May.

**(d)** Disagree. 25% of lobsters needed to be released in June because they are shorter than 8.3 cm, which is higher than the 5.625% released in May. Or 75% of the June catch is within the legal length, compared with 94.375% in May.

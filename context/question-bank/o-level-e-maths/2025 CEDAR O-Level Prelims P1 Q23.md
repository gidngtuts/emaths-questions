## Question

> **Marks:** (a) 1, (b) 3; total 4
> **Page:** 19

A bag contains $12$ counters, $n$ of which are green and the remainder are blue. One counter is drawn at random and not replaced.

**(a)** Write down, in terms of $n$, the probability that the counter is blue. [1]

A second counter is then drawn at random.

**(b)** Given that the probability that both counters drawn are blue is $\dfrac{5}{22}$, form an equation in the form $n^{2}+bn+c = 0$, where $b$ and $c$ are integers. [3]

## Answer Scheme

**(a)** Write down, in terms of $n$, the probability that the counter is blue.

$$
\frac{12-n}{12}
$$

**(b)** Given that the probability that both counters drawn are blue is $\dfrac{5}{22}$, form an equation in the form $n^{2}+bn+c = 0$, where $b$ and $c$ are integers.

$$
\frac{12-n}{12}\times\frac{11-n}{11} = \frac{(12-n)(11-n)}{132}
$$

$$
\frac{(12-n)(11-n)}{132} = \frac{5}{22}
$$

$$
(12-n)(11-n) = 30
$$

$$
n^{2}-23n+102 = 0
$$

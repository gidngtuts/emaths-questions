## Question

> **Marks:** 6
> **Page:** 22–23

The cumulative frequency diagram shows the time in hours that each of the 120 adults from Town A spend on exercise in one week.

![Cumulative frequency curve for 120 adults, time from 0 to 10 hours, cumulative frequency from 0 to 120. The curve rises steeply then flattens, ending at (10, 120).](../../subjects/o-level-e-maths/past-year-papers/2024/ANDSS/images/ANDSS-4E2024EM_Prelim_P1_QP-pages/page-22.png)

**(a)** Explain if you can find the probability of an adult spending exactly 5 hours on exercise weekly. [1]

**(b)** The health promotion board recommends at least $k$ hours of weekly exercise for adults to stay fit and active. $60\%$ of the adults in Town A meet this recommended weekly exercise hours. Find the value of $k$. [1]

The box-and-whisker plot shows the time in hours that each of the 120 adults from Town B spend on exercise in one week.

![Box-and-whisker plot of Town B exercise hours. Whiskers at 0 and 10 hours. The box runs from about 1.6 to about 5.8, with the median near 2.8.](../../subjects/o-level-e-maths/past-year-papers/2024/ANDSS/images/ANDSS-4E2024EM_Prelim_P1_QP-pages/page-23.png)

**(c)** Find the median and interquartile range of the weekly exercise hours of the adults in Town B. [2]

**(d)** Make two comparisons between the weekly exercise hours of the adults from Town A and Town B. State your reasons. [2]

## Answer Scheme

**(a)** No, you cannot, because the cumulative frequency diagram only allows you to find the probability of less than (or less than or equal to) 5 weekly exercise hours, or the probability of at least (more than) 5 weekly exercise hours.

**(b)**

$$
(100-60)\%\times 120=48
$$

$$
k=3
$$

**(c)**

| | Lower quartile | Median | Upper quartile | Interquartile range |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Town B | 1.6 | 2.8 | 5.8 | 4.2 |
| Town A | 1.9 | 3.5 | 5 | 3.1 |

One mark for the correct median and one mark for the correct interquartile range of Town B.

**(d)** On average, the people in Town A have higher weekly exercise hours than Town B since its median is higher.

There is a larger spread in weekly exercise hours in Town B since its interquartile range is higher than that in Town A.

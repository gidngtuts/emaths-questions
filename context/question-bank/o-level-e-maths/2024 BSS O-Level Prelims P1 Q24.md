## Question

> **Marks:** (a) 2, (b) 2; total 4
> **Page:** 17

The sum of the first $n$ terms of a linear sequence is [unclear].

The expression for the sum is not visible on the page.

**(a)** Show that the sum of the first $n$ terms in the sequence is always even.

**(b)** By finding the first three terms in the sequence or otherwise, find, in terms of $n$, an expression for the $n$th term of the sequence.

## Answer Scheme

The sum is not visible on the question paper. The scheme uses $5n^{2}-n$.

**(a)** $5n^{2}-n=n(5n-1)$

When $n$ is odd, $5n$ is odd and $5n-1$ is even.
Since the product of an odd and an even number is always even, $n(5n-1)$ is even. (M1)

When $n$ is even, $5n$ is even and $5n-1$ is odd.
Since the product of an odd and an even number is always even, $n(5n-1)$ is even.

Hence the sum of the first $n$ terms in the sequence is always even. (A1)

**(b)** 1st term: $5(1)^{2}-1=4$

2nd term: $5(2)^{2}-2-4=14$

3rd term: $5(3)^{2}-3-14-4=24$ (M1)

Therefore $n$th term $=10n-6$ (A1)

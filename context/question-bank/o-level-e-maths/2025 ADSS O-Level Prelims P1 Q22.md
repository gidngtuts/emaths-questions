## Question

> **Marks:** [5]
> **Page:** 12

The points $A$ and $B$ are $(-2, 1)$ and $(6, -5)$ respectively. The line $AB$ intersects the $x$-axis at $M$ and the $y$-axis at $N$. $M$ is the point $(p, 0)$ and $N$ is the point $(0, q)$. The line $CD$ is parallel to the line $AB$. The line $CD$ intersects the $y$-axis at $E$. $E$ is the point $(0, p+q)$. Find the equation of $CD$. [5]

## Answer Scheme

Gradient of the line $AB$

$$
= \frac{y_{2}-y_{1}}{x_{2}-x_{1}} = \frac{-5-1}{6-(-2)} = -\frac{3}{4} \quad \text{[M1][B1]}
$$

Equation of $AB$ is $y = -\dfrac{3}{4}x+q$ … (1)

Sub $(6, -5)$ into (1)

$$
-5 = -\frac{3}{4}(6)+q
$$

$$
q = -\frac{1}{2} \quad \text{[B1]}
$$

Thus, equation of $AB$ is $y = -\dfrac{3}{4}x-\dfrac{1}{2}$

When $y = 0$,

$$
0 = -\frac{3}{4}p-\frac{1}{2}
$$

$$
p = -\frac{2}{3} \quad \text{[B1]}
$$

The $y$-coordinate of $E$

$$
= -\frac{1}{2}-\frac{2}{3} = -\frac{7}{6}
$$

Thus, $y$-intercept of $CD$ is $-\dfrac{7}{6}$

Since $CD$ is parallel to $AB$, they have the same gradient.

Thus, the equation of $CD$ is

$$
y = -\frac{3}{4}x-\frac{7}{6} \quad \text{[A1]}
$$

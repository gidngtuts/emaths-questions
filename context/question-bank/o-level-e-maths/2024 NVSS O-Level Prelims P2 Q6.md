## Question

> **Marks:** (a)(i) 1, (a)(ii) 1, (a)(iii)(a) 1, (a)(iii)(b) 2, (b)(i) 2, (b)(ii) 2; total 9
> **Page:** 14–15

**(a)** The first four terms in a sequence of numbers are given below.

$T_{1}=2^{2}$, $T_{2}=2^{4}$, $T_{3}=2^{6}$, $T_{4}=2^{8}$.

**(i)** Find an expression for $T_{n}$.

**(ii)** The first four terms in a second sequence of numbers are given below.

$R_{1}=4^{4}$, $R_{2}=4^{7}$, $R_{3}=4^{10}$, $R_{4}=4^{13}$.

Find an expression for $R_{n}$.

**(iii)** The $n$th term of the third sequence of numbers is given by $Q_{n}=\dfrac{R_{n}}{T_{n}}$.

**(a)** Show that $Q_{n}=2^{4n+2}$.

**(b)** Explain, with working, why 128 is not a term in the sequence $Q_{n}$.

**(b)** $A$ is the point $(4, 12)$ and $B$ is the point $(10, 4)$.

**(i)** Find $AB$.

**(ii)** Given that $\overrightarrow{AC}=\dfrac{1}{2}\overrightarrow{BA}$, find the coordinates of $C$.

## Answer Scheme

**(a)** **(i)** $T_{n}=2^{2n}$ or $T_{n}=4^{n}$ B1

**(ii)** $R_{n}=4^{3n+1}$ or $R_{n}=2^{2+6n}$ B1

**(iii)** **(a)**

$$
Q_{n}=\frac{4^{3n+1}}{2^{2n}}=\frac{2^{2(3n+1)}}{2^{2n}}=2^{6n+2-2n}=2^{4n+2}
$$

B1 (shown)

**(b)** $2^{4n+2}=128=2^{7}$, so $4n+2=7$ and $n=1.25$ M1

128 is not a term because $n=1.25$ is not a positive integer. A1

**(b)** **(i)** $\overrightarrow{AB}=\begin{pmatrix} 6 \\ -8 \end{pmatrix}$, so $AB=\sqrt{6^{2}+(-8)^{2}}=10$ units M1, A1

**(ii)** $\overrightarrow{AC}=\dfrac{1}{2}\overrightarrow{BA}=\begin{pmatrix} -3 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix}$

$\overrightarrow{OC}=\begin{pmatrix} 4 \\ 12 \end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix} -3 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 16 \end{pmatrix}$ M1

$C(1, 16)$ A1

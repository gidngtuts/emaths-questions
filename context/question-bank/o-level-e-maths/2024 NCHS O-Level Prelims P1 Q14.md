## Question

> **Marks:** [3]
> **Page:** 11

The curve $y = \dfrac{1}{5}x^{2}-2x+7$ intersects a line which is equidistant from the positive $x$ and $y$-axes. Find the coordinates of the points of intersection.

## Answer Scheme

$$
\begin{aligned}
y &= \frac{1}{5}x^{2}-2x+7 &&\text{[1]} \\
y &= x &&\text{[2]}
\end{aligned}
$$

Substitute equation [1] into equation [2].

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= \frac{1}{5}x^{2}-2x+7 \\
\frac{1}{5}x^{2}-3x+7 &= 0 \\
x &= \frac{3\pm\sqrt{(-3)^{2}-4\left(\dfrac{1}{5}\right)(7)}}{2\left(\dfrac{1}{5}\right)} \\
x &= 2.89 \text{ or } x = 12.1 \\
y &= 2.89 \text{ or } y = 12.1
\end{aligned}
$$

$(2.89, 2.89)$ and $(12.1, 12.1)$

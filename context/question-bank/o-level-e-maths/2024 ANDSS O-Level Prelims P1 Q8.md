## Question

> **Marks:** 3
> **Page:** 9

The intensity of a sound detected by a receiver is inversely proportional to the square of the distance of the receiver from the source of the sound.
When the distance is $r$ metres, the intensity of the sound detected is $q$ decibels.

When the distance is reduced by $40\%$,

**(a)** find the intensity of the sound detected, in terms of $q$. [2]

**(b)** calculate the percentage difference in the intensity of the sound detected. [1]

## Answer Scheme

**(a)** $q=\dfrac{k}{r^{2}}$, where $k$ is the proportionality constant.
$q$ and $r$ are the initial intensity and distance respectively.

When the distance is reduced by $40\%$, the new distance is $\dfrac{3r}{5}$.

$$
\text{New intensity} = \frac{k}{\left(\dfrac{3r}{5}\right)^{2}}
= \frac{k}{\dfrac{9r^{2}}{25}}
= \frac{25k}{9r^{2}}
= \frac{25}{9}q
$$

**(b)**

$$
\text{Percentage difference}
= \frac{\text{New intensity}-\text{Initial intensity}}{\text{Initial intensity}}\times 100\%
$$

$$
= \frac{\dfrac{25}{9}q-q}{q}\times 100\%
= \frac{16}{9}\times 100\%
= 178\%\ (3\text{ s.f.})
$$

$177\dfrac{7}{9}\%$ is accepted too.

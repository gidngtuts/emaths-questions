## Question

> **Marks:** (a)(i) 2, (a)(ii) 3, (b) 2, (c) 4; total 11
> **Page:** 4–5

**(a)** $2xz=\dfrac{4y-z^{2}}{3z}$

**(i)** Find $y$ when $x=3$ and $z=-1$.

**(ii)** Express $z$ in terms of $x$ and $y$.

**(b)** Solve the inequality $3-x<\dfrac{7-3x}{2}\le 6$.

**(c)** Solve the equation $\dfrac{2}{x+1}+\dfrac{5}{2x-5}=1$.

## Answer Scheme

**(a)** **(i)** $2(3)(-1)=\dfrac{4y-(-1)^{2}}{3(-1)}$, so $y=\dfrac{19}{4}$ or $4.75$

**(ii)** $6xz^{2}+z^{2}=4y$

$z^{2}(6x+1)=4y$

$$
z=\pm\sqrt{\frac{4y}{6x+1}}
$$

**(b)** As printed: $6-2x<7-3x$ and $7-3x\le 12$, so $x<1$ and $-3x\le 5$, and the scheme concludes $x\le -\dfrac{5}{3}$ together with $x<1$.

**(c)** $2(2x-5)+5(x+1)=(x+1)(2x-5)$

$2x^{2}-12x=0$

$2x(x-6)=0$

$x=0$ or $x=6$

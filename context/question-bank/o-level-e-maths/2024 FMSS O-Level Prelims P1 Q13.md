## Question

> **Marks:** (a) 2, (b) 1; total 3
> **Page:** 10

**(a)** Use prime factors to explain why $54 \times 150$ is a perfect square. [2]

**(b)** The number $150k$ is a perfect cube.
Find the smallest possible integer value of $k$. [1]

## Answer Scheme

**(a)**

$$
54 = 2 \times 3^{3}, \quad 150 = 2 \times 3 \times 5^{2}
$$

$$
54 \times 150 = 2^{2} \times 3^{4} \times 5^{2} = (2 \times 3^{2} \times 5)^{2}
$$

Since $54 \times 150 = (2 \times 3^{2} \times 5)^{2}$, $54 \times 150$ is a perfect square.

Or: since the indices of all prime factors are multiples of $2$ (or even), $2^{2} \times 3^{4} \times 5^{2}$ is a perfect square.

M1 for the prime-factorised expression. AG1.

**(b)**

$$
150k = 2 \times 3 \times 5^{2} \times k, \quad k = 2^{2} \times 3^{2} \times 5 = 180.
$$

The smallest possible integer is $180$. B1.

## Question

> **Marks:** 5
> **Page:** 26–27

A liquid container, 30 cm tall, can be modelled by a frustum, A, placed below a cylinder B, as shown in Figure 1.

Figure 2 shows a sketch of the cone that the frustum A is a part of. The diameter $QRS$ of the top of the frustum is 12 cm and the diameter $VUT$ of the bottom is 8 cm. The height of the frustum, $RU$, is 9 cm. $R$ and $U$ are the midpoints of $QS$ and $VT$ respectively.

![Figure 1: cylinder B standing on frustum A. The whole container is 30 cm tall and frustum A is 9 cm tall. Figure 2: cone with top diameter QS = 12 cm, bottom of the frustum VT = 8 cm, frustum height RU = 9 cm, and apex W.](../../subjects/o-level-e-maths/past-year-papers/2024/ANDSS/images/ANDSS-4E2024EM_Prelim_P1_QP-pages/page-26.png)

**(a)** Calculate the volume of the frustum, A. [3]

A liquid is poured into the container at a constant rate of $q\text{ cm}^{3}$ per second.
It takes 50 seconds to fill up A completely and it takes another 30 seconds to fill up B completely.

**(b)** Sketch the height of the liquid in the container with respect to time. Axes are given with height from 0 to 30 cm and time from 0 to 80 seconds. [2]

## Answer Scheme

**(a)** Let the height of the cone in Figure 2 be $h$.

Since triangle $QWS$ is similar to triangle $VWT$,

$$
\frac{h}{h-9}=\frac{12}{8}
$$

$$
12h-108=8h
$$

$$
h=27
$$

$$
\text{Volume of A}
= \frac{1}{3}\pi(6^{2})h-\frac{1}{3}\pi(4^{2})(h-9)
$$

$$
= \frac{1}{3}\pi(36)(27)-\frac{1}{3}\pi(16)(18)
$$

$$
= \frac{1}{3}\pi[684]
= 228\pi
= 716\text{ cm}^{3}\ (3\text{ s.f.})
$$

**(b)** One mark is for the correctly plotted point $(50,9)$. One mark is for the correct shapes for both parts of the graph, from time 0 to 50 seconds and from time 50 to 80 seconds.

The graph starts at the origin, rises as a curve to $(50,9)$, then continues as a straight line to $(80,30)$.

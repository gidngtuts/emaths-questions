## Question

> **Marks:** 6
> **Page:** 20–21

$A$, $B$ and $C$ are points on the circle with centre $O$. $Q$ and $R$ are points on the circle with centre $P$. $CSR$ and $BSQ$ are tangents to both circles. $AOB$ is a straight line.

![Two circles, centres O and P, with common external point S and tangents CSR and BSQ](../../subjects/o-level-e-maths/past-year-papers/2024/HYSS/images/2024-HYSS_S4-5-G3-MA-Prelim_Paper-1_QP-pages/page-20.png)

(a) Show that triangle $CQS$ is congruent to triangle $BRS$. Give a reason for each statement you make. [3]

(b) Angle $ABC = y^{\circ}$. Find, in terms of $y$,

(i) angle $BAC$, [1]

(ii) angle $QPR$. [2]

## Answer Scheme

(a) Since $CSR$ and $BSQ$ are tangents to both circles, tangents from an external point give $CS = BS$ and $QS = RS$. M1.

Angle $CSQ =$ angle $BSR$ (vertically opposite angles). M1.

By SAS, triangle $CQS \equiv$ triangle $BRS$. A1.

Any missing reason: deduct 1 mark from the overall mark for this part. The scheme’s note refers to “Q19”; the working is for question 24(a).

(b) (i) Angle $ACB = 90^{\circ}$ (angle in a semicircle). Angle $BAC = 180^{\circ}-90^{\circ}-y^{\circ} = (90-y)^{\circ}$. B1.

(ii) Angle $OCS =$ angle $OBS = 90^{\circ}$ and angle $PSR =$ angle $PQS = 90^{\circ}$ (tangent perpendicular to radius). Angle $CSB =$ angle $RSQ$ (vertically opposite angles). Quadrilateral $PQRS$ is similar to quadrilateral $OBSC$. Any 2 correct statements used to conclude the quadrilaterals are similar: M1. Reasons are optional for this method.

Angle $OCB =$ angle $OBC = y^{\circ}$ (isosceles triangle). Angle $QPR =$ angle $BOC = (180-2y)^{\circ}$. A1.

Alternative: angle $BOC = (180-2y)^{\circ}$ is B1, and the next B1 is for any correct supporting explanation of that result.

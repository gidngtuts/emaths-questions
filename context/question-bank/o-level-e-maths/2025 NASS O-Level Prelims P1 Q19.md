## Question

> **Marks:** (a) 2, (b) 1, (c) 1; total 4
> **Page:** 16

The sum of the first $n$ terms of a linear sequence is $\dfrac{3}{2}n(n-3)$.

**(a)** Find the first 3 terms of the sequence. [2]

**(b)** Find, in terms of $n$, an expression for the $n$th term of the sequence. [1]

**(c)** Hence, explain why each term in the sequence is a multiple of 3. [1]

## Answer Scheme

**(a)** When $n=1$, $\dfrac{3}{2}(1)(1-3)=-3$

So, $T_{1}=-3$

When $n=2$, $\dfrac{3}{2}(2)(2-3)=-3$

So, $T_{1}+T_{2}=-3$

$$
-3+T_{2}=-3
$$

$$
T_{2}=0
$$

When $n=3$, $\dfrac{3}{2}(3)(3-3)=0$

So, $T_{1}+T_{2}+T_{3}=0$

$$
-3+0+T_{3}=0
$$

$$
T_{3}=3
$$

First 3 terms: $-3$, $0$, $3$

**(b)** $T_{1}=-3$

Common difference $=3$

$$
T_{n}=-6+3n
$$

**(c)** Since $T_{n}=-6+3n=3(n-2)$, each term in the sequence is a multiple of 3.

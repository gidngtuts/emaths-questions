## Question

> **Marks:** (a) 1, (b) 3, (c) 1, (d) 4; total 9
> **Page:** 16–17

**(a)** Complete the table of values for $y = \dfrac{x^{3}}{4}-2x+1$. [1]

| $x$ | $-3$ | $-2$ | $-1$ | $0$ | $1$ | $2$ | $3$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $y$ | $0.25$ | $3$ | $2.75$ | $1$ | $-0.75$ | | $1.75$ |

**(b)** On the grid opposite, draw the graph of $y = \dfrac{x^{3}}{4}-2x+1$ for $-3 \leq x \leq 3$. [3]

![Blank grid for the graph, x from -3 to 3 and a marked y = 1](../../subjects/o-level-e-maths/past-year-papers/2024/CSS/images/Canberra-EM-2024-Prelims-Paper-2-QP-pages/page-17.png)

**(c)** Explain how your graph shows that there is only one solution of the equation $\dfrac{x^{3}}{4}-2x+1 = k$ for some values of $k$. [1]

**(d)** The equation $x^{3}-9x-4 = 0$ can be solved by drawing a suitable straight line on the grid.

**(i)** Find the equation of the straight line. [2]

**(ii)** By drawing this straight line, solve the equation $x^{3}-9x-4 = 0$. [2]

## Answer Scheme

**(a)** $-1$.

**(b)** Not printed in this marking scheme (the graph).

**(c)** From the graph, there is only one intersection point between the line $y = k$ and the curve for some values of $k$ hence there is only one solution for some values of $k$.

**(d)(i)**

$$
x^{3}-9x-4 = 0
$$

$$
\frac{x^{3}}{4}-\frac{9x}{4}-1 = 0
$$

$$
\frac{x^{3}}{4}-2x+1 = \frac{1}{4}x+2
$$

$$
y = \frac{1}{4}x+2
$$

**(d)(ii)** $x = -0.45$ or $-2.75$.

## Question

> **Marks:** (a) 2, (b) 2; total 4
> **Page:** 3

**(a)** Simplify $\dfrac{(4a^{4}b)^{3}}{10a^{3}b^{7}}$, leaving your answer in positive index form.

**(b)** Given that $2^{x} = 3$, $2^{y} = 7$ and $2^{z} = \dfrac{9}{49}$, use the laws of indices to find the value of $2^{3x-y+\frac{1}{2}z}$.

## Answer Scheme

**(a)**

$$
(4a^{4}b)^{3} = 64a^{12}b^{3}
$$

$$
\frac{64a^{12}b^{3}}{10a^{3}b^{7}} = \frac{32a^{9}}{5b^{4}}
$$

**(b)**

$$
2^{3x-y+\frac{1}{2}z} = (2^{x})^{3}\div 2^{y}\times (2^{z})^{\frac{1}{2}} = 3^{3}\div 7\times\frac{9}{49} = \frac{81}{49}
$$

Laws of indices cited on the scheme: $(a^{m})^{n} = a^{mn}$, $a^{m}\times a^{n} = a^{m+n}$, $a^{m}\div a^{n} = a^{m-n}$, $a^{1/n} = \sqrt[n]{a}$.

## Question

> **Marks:** (a) 2, (b) 2; total 4
> **Page:** 14

The base areas of two geometrically similar cones are in the ratio of $9:49$.

**(a)** If the volume of the bigger cone is $1200\text{ cm}^{3}$, find the volume of the smaller cone.

**(b)** 80% of the smaller cone was filled with water. Find the height of water in the smaller cone given that the radius is 3 cm.

## Answer Scheme

**(a)** $\dfrac{l_{1}}{l_{2}}=\dfrac{3}{7}$, so $\dfrac{V_{1}}{V_{2}}=\dfrac{27}{343}$

$V_{1}=1200\div 343\times 27=94.5\text{ cm}^{3}$

**(b)** The scheme says “radius 3” may refer to the water surface or to the cone.

If the radius of the water surface is 3 cm: volume of water $=94.5\times 80\%=75.6$, and $\dfrac{1}{3}\pi(3)^{2}h=75.6$, so $h=8.02\text{ cm}$.

If 3 cm is the radius of the cone: $\dfrac{V_{1}}{V_{2}}=\dfrac{4}{5}$, so $\dfrac{r_{1}}{r_{2}}=\sqrt[3]{\dfrac{4}{5}}$ and $r_{1}=3\sqrt[3]{\dfrac{4}{5}}=2.78495$. Then $\dfrac{1}{3}\pi(2.78495)^{2}h=75.6$, so $h=9.31\text{ cm}$. Both are accepted.

## Question

> **Marks:** (a) 1, (b) 3, (c) 2, (d) 1; total 7
> **Page:** 14

The first four terms of a sequence are given below.

$$
\begin{aligned}
T_{1}&=(2-1)^{2}+5=6\\
T_{2}&=(4-1)^{2}+9=18\\
T_{3}&=(6-1)^{2}+13=38\\
T_{4}&=(8-1)^{2}+17=66
\end{aligned}
$$

**(a)** Find $T_{5}$. [1]

**(b)** Show that the $n$th term of the sequence, $T_{n}$, is given by $4n^{2}+2$. [3]

**(c)** $T_{k}$ and $T_{k+1}$ are consecutive terms in the sequence.

Find and simplify an expression, in terms of $k$, for $T_{k+1}-T_{k}$. [2]

**(d)** Explain why two consecutive terms of the sequence cannot have a difference of 10. [1]

## Answer Scheme

**(a)** $T_{5}=(10-1)^{2}+21=102$ [B1]

**(b)** $T_{n}=(2n-1)^{2}+(4n+1)=4n^{2}+2$

[M1 for $(2n-1)^{2}$; M1 for $(4n+1)$; A1. The scheme prints M2 for the two method points.]

**(c)**

$$
\begin{aligned}
T_{k+1}-T_{k}&=4(k+1)^{2}+2-(4k^{2}+2)\\
&=8k+4
\end{aligned}
$$

[M1, A1]

**(d)** When $8k+4=10$, $k=0.75$.

Since $k$ is not a positive integer, two consecutive terms of the sequence cannot have a difference of 10. [B1]

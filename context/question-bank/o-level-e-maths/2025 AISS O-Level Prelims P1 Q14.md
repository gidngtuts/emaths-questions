## Question

> **Marks:** (a) 1, (b) 3; total 4
> **Page:** 11

$$
n=\frac{m(x-y^{2})}{p},\text{ where }p\neq 0.
$$

**(a)** Find $n$ when $m=14$, $p=9$, $x=2$ and $y=-3$. [1]

**(b)** Rearrange the formula to make $y$ the subject. [3]

## Answer Scheme

$$
n=\frac{m(x-y^{2})}{p},\text{ where }p\neq 0.
$$

**(a)** Find $n$ when $m=14$, $p=9$, $x=2$ and $y=-3$.

$$
n=\frac{14\bigl(2-(-3)^{2}\bigr)}{9}=-\frac{98}{9}
$$

A1, exact value, hence do not accept answers corrected to 3 s.f.

**(b)** Rearrange the formula to make $y$ the subject.

$$
np=m(x-y^{2})
$$

M1 for multiplying $n$ by $p$.

$$
my^{2}=mx-np
$$

$$
y^{2}=\frac{mx-np}{m}
$$

M1 for finding $y^{2}$.

$$
y=\pm\sqrt{\frac{mx-pn}{m}}\quad\text{or}\quad y=\pm\sqrt{x-\frac{pn}{m}}
$$

A1, no marks if $\pm$ is not seen.
